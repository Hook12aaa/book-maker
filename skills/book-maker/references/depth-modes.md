# Book Modes

Two book modes only. Refuse to start without one of the two named explicitly.

## `narrative`

Argument-driven, story arc, personal voice. Research supports the argument but does not dominate the prose. Modelled on books like *The Consultant's Forge*.

- Voice: second-person or engaging narrative. Direct reader address permitted.
- Paragraphs: 3-6 sentences, with transitional bridges between them.
- Chapters: 8-15 across 3-5 parts.
- Citations: endnotes present but unobtrusive. Research supports the argument; it does not lead.
- Stories: at least one scene or anecdote per chapter.
- Metaphors: must be extended or called back. No orphan metaphors.
- Sections flow into each other. The last sentence of one paragraph sets up the first sentence of the next.
- Target: 200-300 pages.

Narrative mode is not fiction. Every factual claim still passes the verification sidecar. The difference is register — the verified facts are woven into an argument rather than presented as a systematic review.

## `research`

Evidence-driven, systematic, dense citations, academic register. Modelled on books like *The Certification Paradox* — but with proper narrative flow across chapters.

- Voice: third-person analytical. No direct reader address.
- Paragraphs: 4-8 sentences, each carrying cited evidence.
- Chapters: 10-20 across 3-6 parts.
- Citations: dense and comprehensive. Minimum 3 cited claims per section.
- Evidence: every factual claim references a verified entry from the claims ledger.
- Analytical voice maintained throughout. No anecdotal opening hooks (use the complication function instead).
- Sections connect through argument, not story. Each section's conclusion raises the question the next section answers.
- Target: 200-300 pages.

Research mode is not a report. Deep-report produces reports; book-maker produces books. The difference is that chapters connect through a narrative spine — each one builds on the previous through transition contracts and thread development. A research-mode book reads as a sustained argument, not a collection of independent analyses.

## Comparison

| Aspect | narrative | research |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | Second-person, personal | Third-person, analytical |
| Reader address | Direct ("you will notice...") | Indirect ("the evidence suggests...") |
| Citations per section | 1-3 (supporting) | 3+ (load-bearing) |
| Opening hook | Scene, anecdote, or question | Complication or evidence |
| Metaphor policy | Required (extended/called back) | Optional (illustrative only) |
| Chapter count | 8-15 | 10-20 |
| Parts | 3-5 | 3-6 |
| Thread density | High (multiple interwoven) | Moderate (argument-linear) |
| Target pages | 200-300 | 200-300 |

## Asking the user

> Which book mode? `narrative` produces an argument-driven book with story arc and personal voice — like a Malcolm Gladwell or Paul Graham book. `research` produces an evidence-driven book with systematic analysis and dense citations — like an academic monograph with proper narrative flow.

If the user names neither, ask again. Do not infer.

## What both modes share

- Verified claims (same sidecar, same source-trust hierarchy).
- Narrative skeleton (spine, architecture, threads, transitions, concepts, argument DAG).
- Sequential chapter drafting (each chapter knows what came before).
- 8 adversarial continuity gates.
- Endnotes per chapter with clickable source URLs.
- Output formats: PDF (via headless browser), ePub3 (for Amazon KDP, Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play), or both.
- Prose quality metrics (Coleman-Liau, hedge density, etc.).

The modes differ in voice, citation density, and structural rhythm. The quality infrastructure is identical. Output format (PDF, ePub, or both) is independent of book mode — both modes support all output formats.
